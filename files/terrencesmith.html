Terrence Smith

The Man Who Lived for Tomorrow
by Musa Tiyiselani

Imagine someone lying to you, and not just telling a simple lie, but lying about something huge that you have believed in your entire life, all for their own benefit. This happens more often than we think, but what if I told you that, in my case, I was actually the one who benefited from the lie?

My name is Terrence Smith, and I have spent most of my life trying to be a perfectionist. I would wake up in the morning, brush my teeth, take a bath, prepare my delicious breakfast and ensure that everything was on schedule. Every morning of my life had a next step, and I wouldn't miss even the smallest detail. One could say I was obsessed with planning, but I always believed that being prepared was better than being surprised.

I spent most of my life ensuring that my next move was perfect and that whatever I did would not jeopardise my future self. I would think about the consequences of my actions before making decisions, sometimes even before I knew what the decision was.

During my teenage years, my mother, who was all I had at the time, once said something that would change the way I looked at life.

"Terrence, I want you to understand something. In this life, you get what you work for, not what you hope for. You can spend your entire life hoping for a better tomorrow, but if you do nothing about today, what exactly do you expect tomorrow to look like?"

I remember those words as though she said them yesterday.

She died a week later.

Those were the last words of hers that I could remember. I grew up without a father, and after my mother's passing, all I had were her words.

You get what you work for, not what you hope for.

This then became my motto, and I made sure that everything I did in life reflected it. I worked hard, planned ahead and made decisions that I believed would benefit my future self. I never wanted to find myself in a situation where I would look back and regret not doing something when I had the opportunity.

However, there was one thing I never considered.

What if, in trying so hard to prepare for tomorrow, I was slowly forgetting to live today?

I would find out soon enough.

It all started on an unusual afternoon when I found myself in a strange little town after a long three-hour walk.

The town was rather interesting. It was noisy, yet somehow quiet. There were people everywhere, but there was something strange about the way they behaved. Some were selling food, others were selling clothes, some were showing off their talents, and others were simply there to watch, all in exchange for gold coins.

Everyone, including the air, seemed to be in motion, except me.

I stood there, looking around, trying to figure out what I was supposed to do next. For the first time in my life, I had no follow-up step. I had no schedule, no instructions and, more importantly, no idea what my next move was supposed to be.

I must say, it was rather uncomfortable.

I tried walking around, hoping to find something familiar, but everything seemed strange. I couldn't even figure out what people were using the gold coins for, let alone where they were getting them.

About half an hour later, the clouds began to darken. It was as though a heavy storm was on its way. A cold wind swept through the town, and people started packing their belongings.

"Is Mother Nature angry with me?" I thought to myself.

I looked up, hoping the clouds would somehow disappear, but things only got worse.

In the blink of an eye, there was no chaos in the town anymore. Everyone but me had vanished.

I looked around, confused.

Where had everybody gone?

I then noticed a heavy rainstorm approaching from the north-eastern side of the town. I had no choice but to run and find shelter before it arrived.

As I was running, I stumbled upon a brown wooden sign with black lettering that read:

COME IN IF YOU WANT TO KNOW YOUR FORTUNE.

The sign was pointing towards an ancient hut that stood alone, away from the town. It was the only shelter I could find, so I decided to enter.

I had never really believed in fortune tellers. I always thought that one's future was determined by the decisions they made, not by some stranger staring at a couple of glowing objects. However, considering the circumstances, I thought it wouldn't hurt to have a look.

Inside, I saw a young, beautiful woman sitting in an ancient-looking brown chair next to a cracked wooden table. On it were two large, glowing marbles that seemed rather magical.

She didn't even look at me when I entered.

Instead, she spoke.

"Ah, the young man who spends his days preparing for something that has not yet arrived."

I stopped.

"Excuse me?"

She slowly raised her head and smiled.

"Come closer, Terrence. There is no need to stand by the door. The rain has not come for you."

I was puzzled.

"How do you know my name?"

She looked at the marbles and replied, "Names are rather easy to discover, young man. It is the things people hide behind them that require a little more understanding."

Weird.

I looked around, trying to figure out what exactly was going on.

"What do you do here?" I asked.

She smiled.

"Some seek knowledge about their future, others seek answers to their circumstances, and a few ask about the afterlife. But you, young man, seek something rather interesting."

She paused.

"And what is this interesting thing I seek?"

She looked directly into my eyes.

"Your coins are all I need to reveal that which is troubling you, so that you may finally understand."

I searched my pockets.

Nothing.

I had been sent by my caregiver to look for a few things in town, and I hadn't brought any money with me.

"I'm afraid I don't have any coins," I said.

She smiled.

"I know."

I was puzzled.

"How did you know that?"

"Your eyes, Terrence. They speak to me. They tell stories that even your mouth has forgotten how to tell."

I wasn't sure what to make of her. Part of me was curious, but another part was telling me to leave before things became any stranger.

"Look, I think I should be going."

"Perhaps you should. But before you leave, allow me to tell you something."

I stopped.

She stared at the two marbles for a moment before speaking.

"Your father loved you, Terrence. More than you could possibly imagine."

I was confused.

"My father?"

"Yes, your father. The man whose existence you have spent your entire life questioning."

I didn't know what to say.

I had never known my father. My mother always told me that he had died when I was too young to remember him.

"How do you know about my father?" I asked.

The woman smiled.

"Your mother told you he was gone, didn't she?"

"Yes. She said he died when I was very young."

The woman tilted her head.

"Interesting. People often mistake the absence of someone for the absence of their love."

I frowned.

"What are you trying to say?"

She looked at me and continued.

"Your father did not abandon you, Terrence. He wanted to be part of your life, but your mother denied him that opportunity. And before he could make things right, he died."

I was shocked.

"What? Are you telling me my mother lied to me?"

She didn't answer.

Instead, she gently touched one of the glowing marbles.

"Your father left you something, young man. Something that would have changed the life you have lived."

"What did he leave?"

"Gold."

I almost laughed.

"Gold?"

"More than you could earn in several lifetimes."

I stood there, trying to digest what she had just told me.

For all those years, I had believed that my mother and I had nothing. I had grown up thinking that my father was simply a man who had never been there for me.

And now a stranger was telling me that he had actually left me a fortune.

"But where is it?"

She smiled.

"Your father left you a letter. He hid it beneath the tree your mother used to take you when you were young. Within that letter lies the key to finding what belongs to you."

I was speechless.

I couldn't understand why my mother would hide something like that from me. Why would she make me believe that my father was a bad person when he had apparently wanted to give me everything?

And if he had left me so much money, why had we struggled for so many years?

"Why would my mother do this?" I asked.

The woman looked at me.

"Ah, young man. Sometimes the people who love us the most are also the ones who make decisions we spend our entire lives trying to understand."

I was about to ask another question when she interrupted.

"However, I have one condition."

"What condition?"

"Promise me you will not betray me."

I stared at her.

"Betray you? How can one betray someone they have only just met?"

She smiled.

"That, young man, is a question you will understand when the time comes."

"Fine. I promise."

She nodded and leaned towards the marbles.

"Then go. But remember, not everything buried is meant to remain hidden, and not everything discovered is meant to be understood immediately."

I didn't know what that meant, but I was no longer interested in the conversation.

I had a father to discover, and possibly a fortune to claim.

I turned towards the door, but before I could leave, she spoke again.

"Terrence!"

I looked back.

"One more thing. When you find what you are looking for, remember that five percent belongs to me."

"Five percent? Of what?"

She smiled.

"Of that which you have not yet found."

I shook my head and left.

Strangely enough, there was no rain outside.

I looked towards the sky, confused, but decided not to waste any more time. I had more important things to worry about.

For the first time in years, I had something to look forward to that wasn't part of my schedule.

✦ ✦ ✦

I spent the next four hours searching for the tree.

I remembered the place quite well. My mother used to take me there whenever she wanted us to spend some time together. It was an enormous tree that stood near the edge of a quiet woodland.

I searched around its roots, examined the branches and even looked inside a few holes in the trunk.

Nothing.

I was beginning to think that the woman had made everything up.

After all, how could a stranger know so much about my family and still expect me to believe everything she said?

Just as I was about to give up, a squirrel appeared.

It climbed the tree and disappeared into a small hole in the trunk. Something about that hole caught my attention.

I moved closer and noticed a brown piece of paper tucked inside.

Could this be it?

I carefully pulled it out.

It looked rather old, and some parts seemed to have been burnt. I opened it and tried reading what was left.

It was a letter.

My beloved child,

I want you to know that I love you more than words could ever explain.

If you are reading this, then perhaps fate has finally changed its course.

Look inside...

All the trouble that I have...

And that was it.

The rest of the letter had been burnt, and I couldn't make out what was written underneath.

I read it again, hoping that perhaps I had missed something.

Nothing.

I looked around the tree, searching for another piece of paper, but there was nothing.

I sat down, holding the letter.

Was this really what my father had left for me? A burnt piece of paper?

Where was the gold?

Had the fortune teller been lying to me?

I had more questions now than answers.

But there was one thing I was certain about. My mother had kept something from me, and I was going to find out what it was.

✦ ✦ ✦

The following morning, I returned to the hut.

The woman was sitting in exactly the same position, with the two glowing marbles still on the table.

I entered and placed the burnt paper in front of her.

"Where is the gold?"

She looked at the paper and smiled.

"Good morning to you too, young man. I trust your search was rather interesting?"

"Please, I don't have time for this. I spent four hours looking for what you told me about, and all I found was this."

She picked up the paper and examined it.

"Ah, the words of a father. Interesting, isn't it? How a few words can outlive the person who wrote them."

I was getting frustrated.

"Can you please tell me where the money is?"

She placed the paper back on the table.

"Tell me something, Terrence. If your father had left you enough money to never work another day in your life, what would you have done?"

I hesitated.

"Well, I would have invested some of it, made sure it grew, secured my future and probably bought a house."

"And after that?"

"I would have made sure I had enough to live comfortably."

"And after that?"

I frowned.

"I don't know. Enjoyed my life, I suppose."

She smiled.

"Interesting. Even when offered a life without financial worries, you immediately begin worrying about the life that comes after it."

I didn't understand what she was getting at.

"Can we please talk about my father?"

She nodded.

"Very well. Your father was a wealthy man. He had worked hard for everything he owned, and he wanted you to have a life that was easier than his. He wanted to give you a beautiful home, a good education and enough money to ensure that you would never have to struggle the way he did."

"Then why didn't I have any of those things?"

"Because your mother refused."

I stared at her.

"She refused?"

"Yes. She believed that giving you everything would take away something that money could never buy."

"What exactly?"

"An understanding of the value of what you possess."

I shook my head.

"That doesn't make sense. Why would a mother deliberately make her child's life difficult?"

The woman smiled.

"Young man, there is a difference between making someone's life difficult and allowing them to understand that life is not always easy."

She paused before continuing.

"Your father wanted to give you the destination. Your mother wanted you to understand the journey."

I looked down at the burnt letter.

"But surely there was a way for them to agree?"

"Indeed, there was. But sometimes two people can love the same person and still disagree about what is best for them."

I remained silent.

Then she asked another question.

"Tell me, Terrence. Have you ever wondered why your mother always reminded you that you get what you work for, not what you hope for?"

"Because she wanted me to work hard and become successful."

"Yes. But was that all she wanted?"

I didn't answer.

She continued.

"Your mother knew about the letter. She knew where your father had hidden it, and she knew what he wanted to give you."

I looked up.

"How?"

"Because she was there when he wrote it."

I was shocked.

"Wait. Are you telling me my mother knew about everything?"

The woman nodded.

"She knew about the fortune. She knew about your father's intentions, and she knew that one day you would come looking for answers."

"Then why didn't she tell me?"

The woman looked at me for a moment.

"Perhaps the better question is, what would you have become if she had?"

I had no answer.

She then pointed towards the paper.

"Look inside, Terrence. Not everything your father left you is written on that piece of paper."

I looked at it again.

"Look inside? What does that even mean?"

She smiled.

"Some answers are not found where we are told to look, young man. Sometimes, we must look beyond the obvious."

I took the paper and left.

✦ ✦ ✦

I returned to the tree that afternoon.

This time, I wasn't searching for gold. I was trying to understand what the woman meant by looking inside.

I examined the paper more carefully. There were burnt edges, a few marks and some words I couldn't quite make out.

I turned it over.

Nothing.

I then remembered something.

The woman had said that my mother knew about the letter. She also said that my father had hidden it beneath the tree.

If my mother knew about it, perhaps she had left something behind too.

I began searching around the tree again, this time paying more attention to the ground.

After a while, I noticed something unusual near one of the roots. The soil seemed to have been disturbed.

I started digging.

A few minutes later, I found a small metal box.

I pulled it out and opened it.

Inside were some old photographs, a small wooden pendant and another envelope.

I picked up the photographs first.

There was one of my mother holding me when I was a baby. Another showed her standing beside a man I had never seen before.

I turned the photograph around.

There was a small message written at the back.

To my beautiful family. May our little boy have a life greater than ours.

I stared at the photograph.

"Could this be my father?"

I opened the envelope.

It was a letter from my mother.

And what I read next made me question everything I had believed about her.

My dear Terrence,

If you are reading this, then I suppose you have finally found what I wanted you to discover.

First, I want you to know that your father loved you. He wanted nothing more than to give you a comfortable life. He wanted you to have everything he never had, and he was willing to do whatever it took to make that happen.

I loved him for that.

However, we did not always agree.

Your father believed that money could protect you from the difficulties of life. He wanted to make sure that you never had to worry about where your next meal would come from or whether you could afford the things you needed.

I understood him, but I was afraid.

I was afraid that if everything came easily to you, you would grow up believing that everything should.

I wanted you to understand the value of hard work. I wanted you to know that there is more to life than having money and owning beautiful things.

Your father and I had many discussions about this. Eventually, he agreed that I should raise you in a way that would allow you to discover your own abilities before discovering what he had left for you.

He did not abandon you, my son. He simply wanted to give you something that I believed you first needed to learn how to appreciate.

The fortune he left you is still yours. I never took it. I only wanted you to find it when you were ready to understand what it meant.

There is something else I want you to know.

I never wanted you to spend your entire life working. I wanted you to work hard, yes, but I also wanted you to enjoy the life you were building.

I remember how you used to wake up every morning excited about the little things. You would play outside, laugh at almost anything and never worry about what the next day would bring.

Somewhere along the way, I hope you never forget that little boy.

My son, please don't turn my advice into a punishment.

You get what you work for, not what you hope for.

But that doesn't mean you should forget to enjoy what you have already worked for.

With all my love,

Mother.

I lowered the letter.

I didn't know whether to cry, laugh or simply sit there and think.

For all those years, I had believed that my mother had hidden the truth from me because she didn't want me to know about my father.

But now I understood.

She had been trying to prepare me for life, just as my father had been trying to prepare a life for me.

They both wanted the best for me. They simply had different ways of showing it.

And perhaps the biggest mistake I had made was taking my mother's words too literally.

She wanted me to work hard, but I had turned hard work into a reason to never stop.

She wanted me to prepare for the future, but I had forgotten that the future was being created by the very days I was neglecting.

I looked at the photographs again.

For the first time, I felt as though I had finally met my father.

✦ ✦ ✦

However, there was still one thing I couldn't understand.

The fortune teller.

How did she know about my father? How did she know about the letter, my mother and even the way I thought?

I had promised her five percent of whatever I found, and I was beginning to wonder whether she had something to do with all of this.

I decided to return to the hut one final time.

When I arrived, she was sitting in the same chair.

I placed the photographs and my mother's letter on the table.

"You knew everything, didn't you?"

She smiled.

"Did you finally find what you were looking for?"

"I found my mother's letter. I know what happened between my parents, and I understand why she did what she did. But how did you know all of this?"

The woman looked at the two marbles.

"Young man, there are things in this world that are easier to understand when one stops trying to control them."

I sighed.

"Please, can you just give me a straight answer for once?"

She laughed gently.

"Straight answers? How boring. You have spent your entire life walking in straight lines, Terrence. Perhaps it is time you learned to appreciate a few turns."

I wasn't amused.

"Who are you?"

She looked at me.

"Tell me something. When you first entered this hut, what did you see?"

"Two glowing marbles."

"And what do you see now?"

I looked at them.

For a moment, the golden light seemed to change. I could almost make out two figures standing inside them.

A man and a woman.

My father and my mother.

The woman continued.

"One wanted to give you everything. The other wanted you to understand the value of anything. Both loved you, and both believed they were doing what was best for you."

She paused.

"But there was something neither of them could have predicted."

"What?"

"That their little boy would grow up and spend his entire life trying to prepare for a tomorrow that neither of them could promise."

I felt a chill run down my spine.

She continued.

"Your father planned your future with his wealth. Your mother planned your future with her words. And you, Terrence, spent your entire life planning your future with your routines."

I stared at her.

"Are you saying my parents made me this way?"

She smiled.

"Did they make you this way, or did you misunderstand what they were trying to teach you?"

I didn't answer.

She leaned back in her chair.

"Your father wanted you to have an easy life. Your mother wanted you to understand that life is not always easy. And you, young man, somehow managed to turn both lessons into a reason to worry about tomorrow."

I looked down.

"Then what am I supposed to do?"

For the first time since I had met her, she stood up.

She walked towards me and spoke gently.

"Tell me, Terrence. Can you change what happened yesterday?"

"No."

"Can you guarantee what will happen tomorrow?"

"No."

"Then why have you spent so much time worrying about things that are beyond your control?"

I had no answer.

She continued.

"Look around you, young man. The sun rises, the birds sing, the trees grow and the seasons change. None of them spend their entire existence worrying about what comes next. They simply do what they are meant to do, one day at a time."

She pointed towards the door.

"You cannot command the sun to rise earlier. You cannot ask the rain to wait for you. And you cannot negotiate with tomorrow before it arrives."

She paused.

"But you can decide what to do with the day that has been given to you."

I looked at her.

"And what about the fortune my father left? Where is the gold?"

She smiled.

"Ah, yes. The gold. I was beginning to wonder when you would remember our little agreement."

I almost laughed.

"You're still expecting your five percent?"

She nodded.

"Of course. A promise is a promise, young man."

I shook my head.

"Fine. But before I go, I want to know something. Who are you really?"

She looked at the marbles one last time.

"Perhaps I am the answer you were looking for. Or perhaps I am simply the question you were never willing to ask."

I frowned.

"What does that mean?"

She smiled.

"You will understand when you stop looking for me."

I looked down at my mother's letter for a moment.

When I raised my head again, the woman was gone.

The two marbles had stopped glowing.

And on the table was a small golden coin.

I picked it up.

There were five words engraved on it.

THE FUTURE BEGINS WITH TODAY.

I looked around the hut, but there was no sign of the woman.

I walked outside.

There was no storm, no strange town and no people exchanging gold coins.

Just an ordinary afternoon, birds singing in the trees and the warmth of the sunlight against my face.

I stood there for a while.

For once, I didn't check the time. I didn't think about what I needed to do next or whether my next move would benefit my future self.

I simply stood there and enjoyed the moment.

And strangely enough, I didn't feel as though I was wasting time.

✦ ✦ ✦

Years later, I would still plan my days, work hard and prepare for the future. I never stopped believing in my mother's words.

But I finally understood what she had been trying to tell me.

Sometimes, we become so obsessed with tomorrow that we forget that today was once tomorrow.

Think about it.

The day you are living right now was once a day you were waiting for. The things you have today were once things you hoped to have. And the opportunities you are enjoying today were once part of the future you were so busy preparing for.

Yet here you are, already thinking about another tomorrow.

I am not saying that you shouldn't plan for the future. In fact, you should. Have goals, work towards them and make decisions that will benefit the person you are becoming.

But don't become so lost in the unknown that you forget what you have control over.

You cannot control everything that happens tomorrow, but you can control what you do today.

You cannot guarantee the outcome, but you can decide the input.

THE FUTURE IS THE OUTCOME, AND THE PRESENT IS THE INPUT.

Whatever you put into today will contribute to the kind of tomorrow you create.

So work hard, but don't forget to rest. Make plans, but don't forget to enjoy the unexpected. Save for the future, but don't forget that you are also allowed to enjoy what you have today.

And most importantly, don't spend your entire life preparing to live.

Because sometimes, in trying so hard to make tomorrow perfect, you end up forgetting that today was once tomorrow.
